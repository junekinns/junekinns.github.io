A Distance-to-Source Baseline and Residual Accuracy for Image-Editing Consistency Judges

Earlier title: The Open Image-Editing Consistency Judge Is Mostly a Distance in Disguise: Residual Accuracy Reveals What It Misses

June Kim1,2
1University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign 2SNOW Corp.
AAAI-27 Student Abstract
Paper (soon) Supplementary (soon) Poster (soon)

Abstract

Judges that score whether an image edit preserved what it should not change (consistency) are validated by pairwise agreement with human preferences. We add a baseline that two such benchmarks omit: pick the output closer to the source, computed with a frozen image encoder. On EditReward-Bench this rule (0.787) is statistically indistinguishable from a dedicated open 8B judge (0.794) and above three other open judges; five of six newer encoders give the same result. On all 3,506 pairs of VCReward-Bench the dedicated judge leads by 7.7pp, mostly on three reference-image tasks. Splitting accuracy by whether the rule is right is more informative than aggregate accuracy: every judge we tested is far more accurate where the rule is right (0.73–0.94) than where it is wrong (0.44–0.77), and judges differ mainly on the latter. On these residual pairs, two API judges whose overall accuracy differs by 1.3pp are 15pp apart. One caveat limits the use of residual accuracy as a training target: on residual pairs the human consistency label usually coincides with the instruction-following label.

Key Findings

  • 1A source-distance rule (DINOv2) is statistically tied with a dedicated open 8B consistency judge on EditReward-Bench (0.787 vs. 0.794). On VCReward-Bench the judge leads by 7.7pp, mostly on three reference-image tasks where the rule is near chance.
  • 2Every judge we tested, open or API, is more accurate on pairs the rule gets right (0.73–0.94) than on residual pairs (0.44–0.77).
  • 3Residual accuracy separates judges that aggregate accuracy does not: Gemini-3.8-Flash and Claude Sonnet 5.5 are 1.3pp apart overall but 15pp apart on residual pairs.
  • 4On residual pairs the consistency winner is usually also the prompt-following winner (87% on labelled EditReward-Bench pairs). Check what residual labels measure before training on them.
